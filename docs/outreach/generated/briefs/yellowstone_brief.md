### 1. Highway Corridor Overview & Road Geometry

Traversing Yellowstone National Park, US-191 & US-89 represents a vital multi-modal transportation corridor carrying high seasonal visitor volumes alongside regional freight and commuter traffic. The evaluated corridor spans 91.0 miles (US-191 MP 18.0-39.5; US-89 MP 0.0-53.5; US-212 MP 0.0-16.0), characterized by winding valley terrain, narrow roadway shoulders, high posted speeds (45–65 mph), and steep topographic cuts that trap wildlife inside the highway right-of-way.

### 2. Focal Wildlife Species & Movement Dynamics

The corridor dissects critical seasonal range and daily movement pathways for flagship North American wildlife:
- **Focal Species:** Grizzly bear (Ursus arctos horribilis), Gray wolf (Canis lupus), Plains bison (Bison bison), Rocky Mountain elk (Cervus canadensis), Moose (Alces alces), Pronghorn (Antilocapra americana)
- **Ecological Phenology:** Spring dispersal toward high-elevation summer pastures coincides with rising summer tourist vehicular traffic; autumn rut migrations force concentrated movements across active roadways during low-visibility dawn and dusk hours.

### 3. Critical Migration Bottlenecks & Collision Hotspots
