### 1. Highway Corridor Overview & Road Geometry

Traversing Olympic National Park, US-101 represents a vital multi-modal transportation corridor carrying high seasonal visitor volumes alongside regional freight and commuter traffic. The evaluated corridor spans 55.0 miles (US-101 MP 200.0-255.0 Lake Crescent & Peninsula Loop), characterized by winding valley terrain, narrow roadway shoulders, high posted speeds (45–65 mph), and steep topographic cuts that trap wildlife inside the highway right-of-way.

### 2. Focal Wildlife Species & Movement Dynamics

The corridor dissects critical seasonal range and daily movement pathways for flagship North American wildlife:
- **Focal Species:** Roosevelt elk (Cervus canadensis roosevelti - world's largest unmanaged herd), Columbian black-tailed deer (Odocoileus hemionus columbianus), Black bear, Cougar
- **Ecological Phenology:** Spring dispersal toward high-elevation summer pastures coincides with rising summer tourist vehicular traffic; autumn rut migrations force concentrated movements across active roadways during low-visibility dawn and dusk hours.
