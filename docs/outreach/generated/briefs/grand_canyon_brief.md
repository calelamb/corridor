### 1. Highway Corridor Overview & Road Geometry

Traversing Grand Canyon National Park, AZ-64 represents a vital multi-modal transportation corridor carrying high seasonal visitor volumes alongside regional freight and commuter traffic. The evaluated corridor spans 71.0 miles (AZ-64 MP 240.0-268.0 Desert View Drive; AZ-67 MP 0.0-43.0 North Rim Parkway), characterized by winding valley terrain, narrow roadway shoulders, high posted speeds (45–65 mph), and steep topographic cuts that trap wildlife inside the highway right-of-way.

### 2. Focal Wildlife Species & Movement Dynamics

The corridor dissects critical seasonal range and daily movement pathways for flagship North American wildlife:
- **Focal Species:** Desert bighorn sheep (Ovis canadensis nelsoni), Rocky Mountain elk (introduced South Rim herd), Mule deer, Mountain lion, California condor (Gymnogyps californianus)
- **Ecological Phenology:** Spring dispersal toward high-elevation summer pastures coincides with rising summer tourist vehicular traffic; autumn rut migrations force concentrated movements across active roadways during low-visibility dawn and dusk hours.
