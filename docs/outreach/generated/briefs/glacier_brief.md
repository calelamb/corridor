### 1. Highway Corridor Overview & Road Geometry

Traversing Glacier National Park, US-2 represents a vital multi-modal transportation corridor carrying high seasonal visitor volumes alongside regional freight and commuter traffic. The evaluated corridor spans 56.0 miles (US-2 MP 142.0-198.0 West Glacier to East Glacier across Marias Pass), characterized by winding valley terrain, narrow roadway shoulders, high posted speeds (45–65 mph), and steep topographic cuts that trap wildlife inside the highway right-of-way.

### 2. Focal Wildlife Species & Movement Dynamics

The corridor dissects critical seasonal range and daily movement pathways for flagship North American wildlife:
- **Focal Species:** Grizzly bear (Ursus arctos horribilis), Canada lynx (Lynx canadensis), Wolverine (Gulo gulo), Bighorn sheep (Ovis canadensis), Rocky Mountain elk, Moose
- **Ecological Phenology:** Spring dispersal toward high-elevation summer pastures coincides with rising summer tourist vehicular traffic; autumn rut migrations force concentrated movements across active roadways during low-visibility dawn and dusk hours.

### 3. Critical Migration Bottlenecks & Collision Hotspots

- **Topographic Drivers:** Riparian confluence zones, highway bends with restricted sight distances, and roadside browse attractions create persistent strike hot spots where wildlife crossing attempts encounter high-speed traffic.
