### 1. Highway Corridor Overview & Road Geometry

Traversing Grand Teton National Park, US-89 / US-191 / US-26 represents a vital multi-modal transportation corridor carrying high seasonal visitor volumes alongside regional freight and commuter traffic. The evaluated corridor spans 28.5 miles (US-89/191/26 MP 180.0-208.5 Moose to Moran Junction), characterized by winding valley terrain, narrow roadway shoulders, high posted speeds (45–65 mph), and steep topographic cuts that trap wildlife inside the highway right-of-way.

### 2. Focal Wildlife Species & Movement Dynamics

The corridor dissects critical seasonal range and daily movement pathways for flagship North American wildlife:
- **Focal Species:** Shiras moose (Alces alces shirasi), Migratory elk (Jackson herd), Pronghorn (Antilocapra americana), Mule deer (Odocoileus hemionus), Grizzly bear
- **Ecological Phenology:** Spring dispersal toward high-elevation summer pastures coincides with rising summer tourist vehicular traffic; autumn rut migrations force concentrated movements across active roadways during low-visibility dawn and dusk hours.

### 3. Critical Migration Bottlenecks & Collision Hotspots

Network Kernel Density Estimation (NKDE) and Getis-Ord Gi* spatial statistics identify severe collision clustering along:
- **Primary Pinch Point:** Moran Junction (US-89/26/191) and Buffalo Valley Road; Gros Ventre River crossing where pronghorn ('Path of the Pronghorn') and mule deer migrate to the National Elk Refuge
- **Topographic Drivers:** Riparian confluence zones, highway bends with restricted sight distances, and roadside browse attractions create persistent strike hot spots where wildlife crossing attempts encounter high-speed traffic.
